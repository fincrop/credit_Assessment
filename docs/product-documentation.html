0.1The rule that governs the whole product

No invented numbers. Every value the system emits traces to a named source — a satellite measurement, a published threshold with a citation, or a farmer-declared field marked as declared. Where we cannot know something, the product says so rather than filling the gap with a plausible-looking default.

This is not a stylistic preference. In a lending product a number gets used: it goes into a credit file, it justifies a decision, and someone is later asked to defend it. A confident wrong number is worse than a stated absence, because an absence invites a question and a wrong number does not. Nearly every design decision in Parts 3 and 4 — the refusal states, the confidence gate, the omitted report panels, the cold peer cohort — follows from this one rule.

Part 1

The product

The problem, the thing we built, and the boundary we will not cross.

1.1The problem

An Indian smallholder farmer applying for a crop loan — a Kisan Credit Card or a comparable agri facility — is usually invisible to conventional credit assessment. There is often no bureau history, no bank statement that reflects farm income, no audited accounts, and no reliable third-party record of what was grown or how it performed. The lender is left with a land record, a declared crop, and a field visit that may or may not happen.

Meanwhile the land itself has been continuously observed from orbit for a decade. Sentinel-2 passes every five days at ten-metre resolution and the archive is free. If a parcel was cropped twice a year for three years, that is visible. If it went fallow, that is visible. If a drought hit at flowering and the canopy never recovered, that is visible. None of it depends on the farmer's paperwork.

The gap is not data availability. It is that raw satellite imagery is not a credit input. Turning it into one requires the parcel boundary to be trustworthy, the vegetation signal to be physically meaningful and comparable between farms, non-farmland to be excluded, cloud gaps to be honestly accounted for, and the resulting number to be explainable line by line to a credit officer and a regulator. That is what this product does.

1.2What we built

A pipeline that takes a farmer identifier and a parcel boundary, reads three years of Sentinel-2 and Sentinel-1 history for that parcel, and returns an explainable agronomic risk index between 300 and 900 — the Krishi Bhoomi Score — together with the evidence behind it.

Around that pipeline sits a working operator application: farmer onboarding with map-drawn or file-uploaded boundaries, an AgriStack integration that pulls government farmer and land records, an assessment dashboard, and a print-ready credit dossier.

1.3What this product is not

This boundary is the most important thing in the document, and it is enforced in code. Every assessment carries positioning: "agronomic_risk_index" and no_repayment_calibration: true, and a test asserts it.

It is notWhy not
A loan amount or credit limitConverting an index into rupees requires a coefficient we can only derive from repayment outcomes. We have none. Any figure would be invented.
A probability of defaultSame reason. The calibration hooks exist in the data model and are deliberately left null.
An approve / reject decisionNo policy layer exists. The lender's own policy sits on top of the index; we do not simulate it.
A crop identification serviceThe classifier is unvalidated and switched off. Crop names come from the registry, are checked against observed phenology, and are always labelled as declared.
A yield forecast in tonnesRelative yield potential from the satellite signal is published science. Converting it to tonnes per hectare for a specific crop and district needs local harvest records we do not have.
Accurate to any published figureWe quote no accuracy number. None has been measured against field truth. See Part 6.
Positioning

The honest sales line is: we tell a lender what the land has actually been doing for three years, with the evidence attached, and we tell them plainly when we cannot see it well enough to say. Everything downstream of that — limit, pricing, approval — stays with the lender until we have repayment data of our own.

1.5The three answers, kept distinct

Most systems collapse everything that is not a score into one failure state. That destroys exactly the information a loan officer needs, because the three cases have three different next actions. The pipeline keeps them apart end to end, from the Python status field to a dedicated screen for each.

Terminal stateWhat it meansWhat the officer does next
SUCCESSWe looked. Here is the score and the evidence.Assess the file.
REJECTED_NOT_AGRICULTURALWe looked. This is not farmland — it is water, built-up, barren or forest, with the spectral evidence attached.Question the land record. Nothing about the farmer is implied.
INSUFFICIENT_DATAWe could not see it well enough to say — the parcel is below the measurable floor, the boundary is untrustworthy, or the window is too cloud-gapped.Re-draw the boundary, or wait for a later observation. This is not a low score.
FAILEDOur system broke.Re-run. Engineering issue, not a finding.

The user interface renders each of these as its own screen with its own evidence, and never shows a greyed-out gauge beside a refusal — an empty dial eventually gets read as a zero.

1.6Where the durable advantage sits

The scoring logic is defensible but reproducible. Three things are not:

  1. The evidence store. Every assessment persists its full per-bin index series, phenology, weather detail, sub-index inputs, weights and version. When field data eventually arrives — verified crops, sowing dates, repayment outcomes — it can be attached retrospectively to assessments already run. Every month spent running without persisting this is a month of ground-truthing opportunity permanently lost.
  2. The peer cohort. A percentile among parcels we have ourselves assessed is a real, self-referential fact that needs volume rather than field work. It compounds: each new assessed parcel improves the comparison for every other parcel in its zone.
  3. The refusal record. Every parcel we reject or flag is a candidate ground-truth sample. Reviewing them is the cheapest available route to a measured false-rejection rate, which is the number a lender will eventually ask for.

2.3Why the AgriStack edge is a separate Lambda in Mumbai

AgriStack's sandbox rejects or degrades traffic from server IPs outside India in both directions: outbound Token and Seek calls return HTML block pages rather than JSON, and inbound webhooks to a foreign sender_uri may be refused. Since the Seek call is made server-side, the IP AgriStack sees is whichever machine runs the Next.js API route — not the operator's browser.

Rather than move the whole application to an Indian host, we moved only the AgriStack surface: a small Lambda in ap-south-1 behind a public Function URL, which proxies Token and Seek outbound and receives the three webhook callbacks inbound, writing them into the same MongoDB collections the application already reads. 3.1Stage 1 — Parcel intake, geometry QA and viability

Everything downstream inherits its spatial footprint and its time window from this stage, which makes it the contract stage of the whole pipeline.

Spatial resolution

The pipeline loads the farmer document from farm_info and, for each parcel, resolves a footprint:

  • Polygon present → validity check plus a comparison of polygon area against the registered hectares. On pass, the bounding box comes from the geometry.
  • Polygon fails QA, or no polygon → fall back to the centroid with an adaptive circular buffer, floored at about 0.15 km.

The fallback is deliberately non-fatal — refusing to score a farmer over a data-entry error would be the wrong trade — but it is not silent. When it happens, the assessment records geometry_source, the risk engine multiplies the confidence gate by 0.85, and the interface draws a full-width banner: the declared boundary greyed and dashed, the measured footprint solid, and the sentence "the score describes the measured area, not the declared one." This was added after a real case where a 0.45 ha holding was scored over roughly 7 ha of surrounding fields.

The viability gate

A Sentinel-2 pixel is 100 m², or 0.01 ha. Two thresholds apply, and they mean different things:

ThresholdValueKindEffect
PARCEL_MIN_PIXELS_HARD15 px = 0.15 haBusinessBelow this, return INSUFFICIENT_DATA before spending imagery quota. Set by lending policy — nobody lends against a plot this small.
PARCEL_MIN_PIXELS_RELIABLE50 px = 0.50 haPhysicalBetween the two, score but flag and discount the gate by 0.85.
PARCEL_AREA_RATIO0.8 – 1.25TrustPolygon area versus registered area. Outside this range, record that they disagree.

The physical threshold has a derivation, not a guess behind it. For a roughly square parcel of N pixels the boundary ring is about 4√N − 4 pixels, and Sentinel-2 geolocation error is itself around ten metres, so boundary pixels are contaminated by whatever is next door: at 15 pixels roughly 73% of the parcel is boundary, at 50 pixels roughly 48%, at 150 pixels roughly 30%. There is no size at which this vanishes for smallholder plots at 10 m resolution. Fifty pixels is where it stops dominating.

On the area ratio the gate deliberately does not decide which number is right. It cannot know from here, and guessing would move the error rather than remove it. It records the disagreement.

Temporal resolution

The lookback is three years, extended backwards to the nearest sowing anchor (15 June, 15 October, 15 February) so a season is never ingested half-clipped. The snap, the anchor used, the window dates, the geometry source and the buffer are all stamped onto assessment.geospatial_prep. A coarse agro-ecoregion is inferred from latitude, longitude and state LGD hints and passed downstream as a soft prior only.

3.2Stage 2 — Satellite acquisition and signal construction

This is the most expensive stage and the one everything else depends on.

Acquisition

  • Optical: Sentinel-2 L2A via Earth Engine by default, with the Planetary Computer STAC as fallback. Up to 120 scenes per calendar year are fetched and sorted before binning.
  • Radar: Sentinel-1 GRD, VV and VH, ascending preference, used to fill monsoon cloud gaps.
  • Cloud masking: Cloud Score+ (cs_cdf, threshold 0.60) combined with SCL and QA60. Version stamp csplus_scl_v2.
  • Binning: fixed 10-day bins across the window. The lowest-cloud scene wins each bin. Empty bins are marked missing rather than interpolated away.
A defect worth remembering

The scene cap was previously a hardcoded 50 per year applied without sorting, so the pipeline took the chronologically first 50 scenes and discarded the rest of the year. That produced apparent 240-day cloud gaps and pushed genuine farms into INSUFFICIENT_DATA. The gaps were ours, not the weather's. This is why every provider-level parameter is now named, defaulted deliberately and documented in configuration.

Signal construction — the highest-leverage fix in the backend

The composite vegetation signal is version-stamped signal_v2_fixed_range. Version 2 changed two things, and both were correctness fixes rather than tuning:

Normalisation: per-parcel min–max became fixed physical ranges. Version 1 rescaled each index over that parcel's own three-year extremes. Every parcel — fertile, barren or paved — therefore produced a series spanning roughly 0 to 1, so absolute thresholds applied to it were meaningless, peak_cvi was not comparable between two farms, and noise on dead ground was stretched into apparent crop cycles. Version 2 normalises against published physical ranges per index (NDVI −0.20 to 0.90, NDMI −0.50 to 0.60, and so on). Clipping is now meaningful: NDVI of −0.4 over water clamps to zero, which is to say no vegetation.

Backbone: kNDVI became NDVI. kNDVI is tanh(NDVI²), which discards the sign of NDVI — open water at −0.30 and sparse crop at +0.30 both map to 0.0876. Carrying that at weight 0.50 made the detection signal structurally unable to tell water from vegetation. kNDVI is still computed and stored, because it is a good vigour index; it is simply not the discrimination backbone. The blend is now NDVI 0.50, EVI 0.30, NDMI 0.20.

Optical–radar fusion and quality

quality = f(valid_pixel_fraction, cloud_probability, parcel_size)   ∈ [0,1]
VS      = quality · VS_optical  +  (1 − quality) · VS_radar

every bin carries  signal_source ∈ {optical, fused, sar, imputed}
                   bin_quality   ∈ [0,1]

Smoothing is a Whittaker second-difference smoother. Imputed bins are flagged so that a reconstructed value can never be mistaken for an observed one — a rule the interface enforces visually as well, by drawing markers only on directly observed bins.

Hard limits: fewer than 12 valid bins aborts the run; under 20 raises a warning.

3.3Stage 2b — The land-cover gate

Before this module existed the pipeline had no land-cover logic whatsoever. It accepted any valid polygon anywhere on Earth and emitted a risk index for it. Traced end to end, a parking lot produced index 41, category HIGH, status SUCCESS — and dense forest scored better than a genuine fallow farm, because the stability sub-index rewards the absence of stress anomalies and nothing stressful happens to a forest.

A lender cannot use a score that might have been computed over a lake. This gate is the single most important correctness feature in the product.

Confidence bands, not a boolean

The gate emits a calibrated confidence, and three outcomes follow:

ConfidenceOutcome
≥ 0.75 and non-agriculturalREJECTED_NOT_AGRICULTURAL, no score, evidence attached

Classes are CROPLAND / WATER / BUILTUP / BARREN / FOREST / PLANTATION / UNKNOWN. PLANTATION is a distinct non-rejecting verdict and must never be collapsed into forest or barren — orchards, banana and ratooned sugarcane are products we want to score. The verdict is stamped on every assessment, including passes, so a lender can see the evidence either way.

Open item — and it is on the critical path

Third-party LULC is implemented but LANDCOVER_USE_EXTERNAL_LULC is False. The gate is deliberately held closed until someone reads and records the published per-class accuracy for the cropland class over South Asia in configuration. Ten-metre global products are weakest on sub-hectare fields, which is exactly our population, so the headline global accuracy figure must not be assumed to apply. This is a half-day of reading that unblocks the strongest evidence stream we have.

3.4Stage 4 — Phenology, cycles and seasons

With crop classification off, cycle detection is the crop-agnostic backbone that feeds weather, performance and most of the score.

Annual detection

The smoothed composite signal is walked for peaks, then backwards to a sowing point and forwards to a harvest point, with amplitude and base gates rather than brittle absolute thresholds. Soft priors are applied where available: the agro-ecoregion nudges the peak gate within bounds relative to the configured threshold, and a declared sowing date biases candidate selection within about ±21 days. Neither is a hard override, and both are recorded in hints_applied and applied_knobs.

What the drift harness caught here

The agro prior originally clamped the peak gate to an absolute range of 0.20–0.35, written for the old per-parcel signal scale. On the new physical scale, 0.35 is bare soil — so the prior was silently re-creating the threshold inversion the signal rewrite had just removed. The bounds are now relative to the configured threshold. Unit tests passed throughout; only a run against real data exposed it.

The perennial branch

Orchards, banana and ratooned sugarcane never return to bare ground. With no trough, the annual sow–peak–harvest walk always exceeds the duration cap and every candidate is rejected — which produced zero cycles, which downstream read as cropping intensity zero and fallow 1.0. A mango grove was being scored as abandoned land.

The fix treats "no trough" as a signal rather than a failure. When a canopy is present year-round (p10 of the signal above 0.50, bare fraction under 0.05, limited seasonal amplitude), the parcel routes to a perennial cycle model, and the land-use sub-index uses a perennial branch where canopy persistence and inter-annual vigour stability are the credit signal rather than cycle count. A real orchard now returns three production years, 90.7% land utilisation and an index of 75.5.

Seasons

A cycle is assigned to the season it spent the most days in, not the one it happened to be sown in; below a 55% dominant share it is labelled cross_season. Kharif, Rabi and Zaid are all first-class. South Indian calendars are shifted 30 days where latitude is below 16°N, which is the coarsest defensible regionalisation and an obvious improvement target once a state-level calendar is available.

Land utilisation is reported per season with the invariant that utilisation plus fallow equals one.

3.5Stage 5 — Crop context, handled carefully

There were two bad options here and one good one.

Trusting the registry crop routes the parcel down the crop-specific scoring path, where it is benchmarked against ICAR reference curves. A wrong self-declaration then swings up to 45% of the index on nothing. Ignoring it is what the pipeline did: crop confidence was hardcoded to 0.0 against a 0.25 gate, so a declared crop set a label and nothing else — leaving roughly 1,150 lines of ICAR reference data dead and the per-stage weather analysis permanently inert, because it needs a crop name.

The third option needs no ground truth: check the declaration against what we saw. Cotton is documented at 165–180 days with a characteristic trajectory. If the observed cycle ran 90 days and peaked low, the declaration is inconsistent with the observation, and we can establish that from data we already have.

Verification outcomeEffect
ConsistentUnlock crop-specific scoring at a bounded confidence — a self-report that survives a check is still a self-report
InconsistentCrop-agnostic scoring, and declared_crop_mismatch recorded with the evidence
IndeterminateCrop-agnostic scoring, no claim either way

Two consequences follow. The crop name is always rendered as "Cotton (farmer-declared, unverified)", never as "Cotton". And the mismatch flag is the first crop-label signal we will ever have collected — it is training data for the classifier we cannot yet retrain.

The ML classifier is quarantined. It is off by default, its flag is named experimental, it logs loudly when enabled, it stamps crop_label_source: ml_experimental_unvalidated, and it now refuses to load if its feature count disagrees with the model artefact. Its embedded 95.8% accuracy figure is never surfaced: it was almost certainly inflated by a train/test split that was not grouped by farm, and a leakage-inflated figure shown to a lender is worse than no figure.

3.6Stage 6 — The weather engine

Weather is a full pillar at 25% of the index, not a footnote. It runs in two directions.

DirectionQuestionWhy it matters for credit
Backward — resilienceDid this parcel's vegetation hold up through past adverse weather?This is the high-value signal: it is observed behaviour under stress, not a forecast.
Forward — exposureHow drought, flood and heat prone is this zone?Contextualises the risk the next season carries.

Indicators are computed per detected cycle and aligned to growth stages, so "heat stress at flowering" is a first-class reason code rather than a seasonal average: SPI and SPEI-style standardised anomalies, dry-spell and wet-spell run lengths, growing degree days with heat and cold stress days, and monsoon onset timing anomaly.

Source priority is configurable. IMD gridded, ERA5-Land and CHIRPS fetchers are implemented; only NASA POWER is enabled today. Responses are cached in Mongo and in memory, and the result carries weather_data_status of ok, degraded or unavailable so a consumer can distinguish missing data from low risk.

3.7Stage 7 — Vigour, yield potential and stress

Yield potential is the integral of NIRv over the cycle — a published biomass and productivity proxy — expressed as a potential_index from 0 to 100, with an empty quantified_yield slot reserved for the day a crop coefficient can fill it. The distinction is deliberate: NIRv-AUC as a relative yield-potential proxy is published science; converting it to tonnes per hectare for a specific crop and district needs a local coefficient we cannot derive. The relationship is real; the calibration constant is not.

Stress is measured against the parcel's own three-year, per-growth-stage distribution rather than a within-cycle spread, with departures flagged by percentile fence. Impact is stage-weighted: flowering and grain-fill count far more than vegetative or senescence. Water stress comes from LSWI and NDMI drops. Anomaly events are persisted individually, so the narrative can say "water-stressed on 14 August 2023 during flowering" rather than collapsing to a count.

3.8Stage 8 — The risk index

This is where the signals become a number. The engine is deterministic, rule-based and fully attributable; there is no learned model in the critical path.

The arithmetic, in full

additive   = Σ ( sub_index_score_i × weight_i / 100 )     over the four substantive pillars
raw_index  = clip( additive + benefits_bonus , 0 , 100 )
gate       = data_confidence_gate  ∈ [0.60, 1.0]
             × 0.85 if the land-cover verdict was flagged
             × 0.85 if the footprint was substituted
             × 0.85 if the parcel is between the fundable and reliable size floors

Note the gate floor. Even a parcel with very poor observation quality retains 60% of its raw index; the gate annotates and discounts, it does not zero out.

Previously they defaulted to 50 and 84 respectively — stability rewards the absence of stress events, and nothing stressful happens to a parking lot — so barren land scored well on its two strongest pillars. "We observed nothing growing here" is a negative finding, not a missing one. It is paired with the data-sufficiency check below so that ignorance is never reported as a finding.

3.9Data sufficiency — refusing to conclude from a blind window

This check exists because of a real result. The first drift run split cleanly into two groups: parcels where cycles were detected moved by a mean of −8.6 points (a healthy correction), while parcels with zero cycles moved by −50.5, all landing near 26 and VERY_HIGH. One of those parcels had two 240-day stretches with no usable optical observation at all — 41% of the window blind.

Both the old and new numbers were wrong. The old pipeline manufactured cycles from noise. The new one correctly found none — then reported a confident VERY_HIGH for a parcel it could barely see. You cannot conclude "nothing grew here" from a period you did not observe, and a 240-day gap is longer than any annual crop cycle: an entire season could have been sown, grown and harvested inside it.

The rule is physical, so it needs no ground truth to justify: if no cycles were detected and the record contains a contiguous unobserved gap longer than the shortest crop cycle we would recognise, the outcome is INSUFFICIENT_DATA, not a low score.

3.10Farmer-level aggregation

Satellites see land; credit goes to a person. A farmer with five parcels gets one score, and it is not an average.

3.11Stage 10 — Explainability

Four layers, in descending order of how much we trust them:

  1. Reason codes. Deterministic, structured, conditional on the input that justifies them. This is the adverse-action layer.
  2. Driver captions. One grounded sentence per sub-index, built from that sub-index's own inputs by deterministic template. Deliberately not generated: an LLM asked to write these produces fluent sentences containing invented figures.
  3. Counterfactuals. Score-improvement scenarios computed as index deltas that are dimensionally correct against the scoring formula. No external API.
  4. Narrative. Optional Groq generation, optional Sarvam translation into Indian languages, with the model id and a prompt hash persisted alongside the text so a narrative shown to a loan officer is reproducible after the response ends. Both are off unless keys are set; a deterministic rule-based narrative is the fallback.

Two honesty repairs sit here. The narrative surface previously described vigour as "peer-relative" and reason codes claimed "above peers" while no peer comparison had ever occurred — the cohort writer had no caller, so the benchmark permanently cold-started. That language is gone until the cohort is warm. And the SHAP-style attribution now reports shap_available: false on the heuristic path rather than claiming a computation it does not perform.

3.12Peer benchmarking

Built, cold. A percentile among parcels we have assessed in the same agro-climatic zone is a real, self-referential fact — it needs volume, not field data. The feature store has been accumulating the raw material on every run; the aggregation job that populates cohort statistics now exists and runs.

It stays inactive until a zone reaches 20 assessed parcels, because below that a percentile is an artefact of who happened to be onboarded first. Two honesty constraints are permanent: state the cohort size wherever a percentile appears, and describe it as "compared to N parcels assessed in this zone" — never as a population statistic, because our assessed set is not a random sample of Indian farms.

3.13Stage 11 — Persistence

Three writes per run: the assessment, the evidence, and a score-history append. Together they mean a six-month-old assessment is retrievable and comparable, a parcel has a queryable score history, and the full index series behind any stored score can be recovered.

The reasoning is not only about the report. You cannot retrospectively validate what you did not store. When field data arrives, this is what makes it usable against assessments already run — which is the single highest-leverage thing the current architecture does for the next twelve months.

4.5The two panels that carry the most weight

The score waterfall — why this number

Four sub-index bars used to sit beside an orphaned 0.92. They now show the arithmetic:

  • Bars encode contribution, not score. Each track is sized to its weight, so the four together span the full 100 points a raw index can reach, and each is filled to its contribution. A sub-index of 42 matters differently at weight 30 than at weight 20, and four equal-length bars could never show that.
  • The unfilled part of a track is the recoverable loss — which is the number a loan officer actually acts on.
  • The gate is a step in points, not a multiplier in a box. And where the footprint was substituted, the engine folds that discount into the gate in place, so it is named inside the gate row rather than drawn as a separate step, which would misstate the formula.
  • The clip is named. When the raw index clips to range, a row says so. Silently showing parts that add to a different total looks like an arithmetic error.

Nine assertions in the smoke suite pin this arithmetic against the Python engine's, including that four all-100 drivers reach exactly 100 — otherwise the tracks would imply unreachable headroom.

The NDVI trajectory — what we actually saw

Three rules, each load-bearing:

  • Gaps stay gaps. No line is drawn across a bin with no observation; the blind span gets a hatched band and a count. A line through a cloudy fortnight asserts a measurement that was never taken.
  • Provenance is texture, not colour. Optical solid, fused dashed, radar dash-dot, reconstructed dotted. It is the same quantity at different confidence; a colour change would imply a different measurement, and colour is reserved for magnitude.
  • Markers only on directly observed bins. A dot asserts "we saw this", so reconstructed values do not get one.

The y-axis is floored at zero rather than at the series minimum, which would exaggerate small variation into a dramatic curve. And there is no synthesised district median — until a cohort is warm, the parcel's own line is drawn alone with the reason stated.

4.8Design system

The visual direction is an editorial dossier: cream paper, hairline rules, warm shadows, generous type, near-zero imagery. It should read as a credit document and print as one.

Imagery earns its place only when it is evidence — the Sentinel-2 basemap under a parcel boundary. A photograph of a wheat field behind a login form is the visual equivalent of a fabricated data point: it implies a richness the screen is not delivering. The texture comes from paper, rule weight and typography.

One colour, one meaning

There were five competing score-to-colour ramps in the app, so an index of 68 could render amber, green and "Good" simultaneously depending on which component drew it. All five are now one function. The band definitions carry a mark colour, a text-safe ink step, a tinted surface and a border, and the backend risk category maps onto the same four bands so a risk pill and the gauge cannot disagree.

The four band hexes were validated rather than chosen — run through a palette validator against the cream surface for lightness banding, chroma floor, colour-vision-deficiency separation and contrast. The previous set failed: the two bands that decide fundability measured ΔE 8.8 for normal colour vision, meaning they were not reliably distinguishable.

Colour is never the only channel

A red–amber–green scale is structurally unsafe under protanopia; no re-stepping fixes it, because the amber-green pair collapses. So every band renders with its name, and on the gauge with its position on the arc. A bare coloured chip with no adjacent word is forbidden.

The score drift harness

Every correctness fix in the pipeline changes scores. "We fixed it, trust us" is not something to put in front of a lender, so the drift harness answers concretely: how much moved, in which direction, for whom, and driven by which pillar. It compares the most recent stored assessment written by the old code against a fresh run of the current code — no need to keep the old pipeline runnable.

It should run before every release, because it has already caught three defects the unit tests missed:

  1. The agro prior was dragging the peak gate down to bare soil, on an absolute clamp written for the old signal scale.
  2. Three discriminator indices never reached the land-cover gate, because a hand-written key list in the binning step silently dropped every index added after it.
  3. The no-evidence floor never fired. Its test asserted it and passed — because the fixture omitted a key the real analyser always supplies. A test that only exercises the shape it invented is not a test of the pipeline.

The report contract test

It checks both directions — backend keys the frontend does not type would be silently dropped data; typed keys the backend never sends would render as undefined — and pins the things a renderer must not get wrong: that the scale is 300–900, that positioning is the agronomic index, that the four band names match the gauge, that the omitted panels are still named, and that the backend's KBS equals the frontend's own index-to-KBS mapping. 6.4The binding constraint is the ingest, not the pipeline

This is the number one item in the company

A geometry audit over the live database — 19 farmers, 113 parcels — found the parcel records themselves are largely unusable, and the split by source is total.

SourceParcelsArea ratio in toleranceMedian sizeViable at 0.15 ha
Application-drawn77 / 71.59 ha6 viable, 1 marginal, 0 refused
AgriStack-ingested1067 / 1060.11 ha1 viable, 51 marginal, 54 refused

Polygon-versus-registered area ratios span 0.022 to 1841 in both directions. A unit-conversion error would cluster on one constant; this scatter means the polygon and the registered area are describing different parcels. The worst observed case had 0.0036 ha registered against a 6.63 ha polygon.

No amount of pipeline work fixes this. The scoring pipeline can now detect and refuse these parcels — which is precisely why the problem became visible — but it cannot repair the pairing. The fix belongs in the AgriStack ingest path: the Lambda and the ingest module in the application.

Two consequences follow, and both are commercial rather than technical. First, a pilot should be run on application-drawn boundaries, where the data is good, rather than on bulk AgriStack ingest. Second, the ingest defect must be diagnosed before AgriStack is presented as a data-acquisition advantage, because today it supplies one usable parcel in a hundred.

6.5Live gaps, in order of how much they matter

  1. AgriStack ingest quality (§6.4). Caps everything downstream.
  2. Crop-specific analysis rarely runs. The classifier is quarantined, so a crop name only arrives via the registry — and only unlocks crop-specific paths when phenology verification passes. Where it does not, the per-stage weather analysis contributes nothing and a large body of ICAR reference data stays dormant.
  3. Peer benchmarking is cold and stays cold until parcel volume improves. A percentile needs 20 assessed plots per zone; there are currently seven viable parcels in total.
  4. Third-party LULC is disabled pending the published-accuracy record. This is the strongest land-cover evidence stream and it is switched off by a documentation task.
  5. Radar reliance is visible but unvalidated. The radar-to-signal mapping is fitted on clear-season bins and extrapolated onto monsoon radar. The reliance is now recorded per assessment; its accuracy is not established.
  6. Nothing has been visually verified. No screen has been rendered against a real assessment in a credentialed environment. The interface is backed by type checks, 98 assertions and a successful build — not by looking. The dual-footprint map and the NDVI trajectory are the two most worth a manual pass before a pilot.
  7. Structural debt: two schemas still coexist in the assessment collection, the queue collection is still the de-facto payload store, and the dashboard page still holds job orchestration inline.

6.6What would make it verified

One thing, and it is cheap: a manual review queue over rejections and zero-cycle outcomes. Every parcel a human eyeballs against high-resolution imagery is a ground-truth sample we did not have yesterday, and it is the only route to a measured false-rejection rate. It should start with the first operational batch, before it is needed.
